Kanpèch

A Haitian kitchen in Pétion-Ville. We cook over wood fire five evenings a week, and for Sunday lunch.

Open Tuesday to Saturday from 6 pm, and Sunday from noon.

Book a tableSee the menu

Named after a tree

Kanpèch is the Kreyòl name of the logwood tree. Its heartwood gives a dye that runs from red-violet to black, and for a long time it left Haiti’s ports by the shipload to colour cloth and ink abroad.

We kept the name, and the colour.

Three dishes to start with

  1. Ke bèf an sòs

    Oxtail braised for six hours with epis and thyme, served with diri ak djon djon.

  2. Pwason boukannen

    A whole snapper over the coals, with sour orange and ti‑malice sauce.

  3. Bannann peze

    Green plantain, pressed and fried twice, with a bowl of pikliz.

The room

Forty seats under low red light, a long table for eighteen, and a courtyard for warm nights. The kitchen is open to the room, so you can watch the fire.
