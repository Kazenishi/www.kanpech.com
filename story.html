Named after a tree

Kanpèch is the Kreyòl name of logwood, a small, twisted tree that grows across Haiti’s dry hills.

Its yellow flowers feed the bees, and logwood honey is one of the best on the island.

The colour inside

Cut open, its heartwood is orange, and it darkens in the air. Boiled, it gives a dye that goes from red-violet to deep black, depending on what you mix it with. For a long time logwood was one of the country’s exports, sent out by the shipload to colour cloth and ink.

We took its name, and the colour of its heartwood, for a restaurant that cooks the way many Haitian kitchens still do: slowly, with fire.

How we cook

Every morning starts with epis: parsley, scallions, garlic, thyme and peppers pounded together. Almost everything on the menu meets it at some point.

Meat is marinated in sour orange the day before. Rice is cooked in the pot until it sticks a little at the bottom, the way people fight over at home.

The fire

The grill sits at the back of the room, in view of every table. It is fed with prunings from mango and citrus orchards, never with freshly cut trees.

Fish, goat, corn and conch go over the coals. Stews and rice stay on the stove, slowly.

The room

Dark wood, red-violet walls and a candle on every table. Forty seats inside, a long table for eighteen, and a courtyard when the night is warm.
